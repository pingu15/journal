# Journal

A premium, minimal, editorial journaling app. Native iOS, Swift + SwiftUI, **iPhone only**.

## Design direction
- **Style:** minimal / editorial. Lots of whitespace, restrained color, no ornament. Type: **serif in exactly three places** (New York via `design: .serif`): page titles (`LargePageTitle`: the journal's name and Recently Deleted; the lock screen), the day numbers in entry lists (`Typography.dayNumber`) and the Insights tiles' numbers. **Everything else in the app is sans (SF)**: other titles, sheet and popup titles, entry text. Never add serif anywhere else. The exported PDF (`JournalPDF`) is the one holdout, still typeset in serif.
- **Palette:** https://colorhunt.co/palette/3434348e8b82e9dcbef3f3f3
  | Token | Hex | Role |
  |---|---|---|
  | ink | `#343434` | Primary text (light) |
  | stone | `#8E8B82` | Secondary text, metadata, hairlines, Markdown syntax |
  | sand | `#D4B48C` | The single accent — button fills, highlights (always white text and icons on top, `Theme.onAccent`) |
  | paper | `#F3F3F3` | Background (light) / primary text (dark) |
  | night | `#121212` | Background (dark) — deliberately deeper than ink (not from the palette link) |
- Use semantic tokens from `Journal/Design/Theme.swift` (`Theme` for SwiftUI, `UITheme` for UIKit, `Typography`, `Spacing`, `Radius`) — never hardcode colors, fonts, or spacing in views.
- `Typography` is the app's whole type scale: every view takes its font from it, by role, and a new role gets a token rather than a one-off, except Settings, which keeps iOS's own fonts throughout (its section headers and footers are the Form's, not `MetaLabel` or `Typography.note`). Section labels are otherwise always `MetaLabel` (months, Insights, the day sheet, Set Location); popup titles are `Typography.title`, but a sheet's bar title (Settings, Set Location, Entry Date) keeps the system's size.
- Titles: every tab applies `.pageTitle(_:isFullScreen:)` (`PageTitle` in `Design/Components.swift`) to its scroll view: the journal's name and year span (`LargePageTitle`, white on a sand band that runs up behind the bar) stay fixed, and the page's content scrolls in a card with rounded top corners below it (its scroll bar starts below the corners). A grabber (`Grabber`, a thin stone capsule; every sheet but Settings shows the same one via `.sheetGrabber()` in place of the system's indicator; both go through `.sheetBackdrop()`, which darkens the page's card behind the sheet past the system's dimming, down over the tab bar but not the title above it, with `SheetBackdrop` under the sheet, placed from the `PageCardMarker` in what presented it, or over the whole window without one, as from an open entry) at the card's top (its touch area only 120pt wide, so touches beside it reach the content: the top of the map stays usable) drags it up to full screen — just below the notch, over the title and the bar — and back down, following the finger; only a drag moves it (no tap; VoiceOver gets an action). Every tab's card is in the same place: leaving one at full screen or down leaves them all there (`PageCards`, for the session; `PageTitle.matchOtherPages` when a tab appears). The bar is hidden (`.toolbar(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)`) whenever the card is off its resting place, so its buttons go as the drag starts and return the moment the card is back, even with the finger still down (`PageTitle.follow`). Its space vanishes at once, so the card is shifted by the bar's height unanimated (`move(up:)`, which on release lets the card settle with a short bounceless animation before the bar returns), and the title stays in the layout, opaque and offset by the bar's height while it's hidden, so the card just slides over it and off it (no fade). Every tab's content starts `Spacing.l` below the card's top, leaving the grabber room (the Calendar's weekday row has that top padding, and the journal's label's `Spacing.m` below it, so its line sits at the height of the journal's first one). Every tab shows the journal name — only Recently Deleted passes its own title (no year span, but its line stays blank, so the card rests at the same height). The bar has no title (an empty principal item keeps the system from drawing one); it has no background of its own — the page's sand shows through, so it slides away with the page when an entry is swiped closed (a bar background switched to sand at once) — and `toolbarColorScheme(.dark)` so its buttons are white in both modes (the status bar picks its own color from the sand).
- Home: searching holds the card at full screen (`isFullScreen: searchActive`, no grabber) with `ExpandingSearchBar` over the card's top, in the card's colors. Month / "On this day" labels (`SectionLabel`) are ordinary rows; the label of the section at the top is drawn again over the list (`PinnedSectionLabel`), placed from each label row's reported position (`LabelTops`, kept relative to the list when reported: rows report only when the list scrolls, not when the card around it moves), so it stays at the top while its rows scroll under it and is pushed straight up — never faded — by the next. (The list's own pinned section headers faded out under the rows as the next one arrived.) The overlay is clipped to the list and lets touches through; `listSectionSpacing(0)`, and rows force their bottom separator. The list hides iOS 26's top scroll-edge effect (`hidesTopScrollEdgeEffect()`) so rows pass under the label with a clean edge. Row separators all start at one point (`EntriesView.contentInset`, via `.alignmentGuide(.listRowSeparatorLeading)`) and the month labels start where they do; rows and their separators end the same distance in from the right — 20pt from the screen edges, like Insights and the Calendar. Every label sits `Spacing.l` below the card's top edge or the separator above it and ends, `Spacing.m` below its text, in a separator of its own, above the top of its first row (`SectionLabelContent`); the pinned copy has no line and stops just above the row's, so that line shows while the label is in place and scrolls away under the copy with the rows. The label's background stops `Spacing.s` short of the right edge, so it doesn't cover the scroll bar, and Insights' first tile starts at the height of that line. Rows have no list insets: they keep them inside the row (`entryRowPadding`; `EntriesView.rowInset` = the list's own 15 above and below), so the highlight spans the screen and runs from separator to separator. Search: the top-left button hands off to `ExpandingSearchBar` (not a toolbar item — a field inside one can't span the bar; iOS collapses it into a ⋯ overflow menu), drawn over the page (`belowNotch()`), not in the card, so one circle travels the whole way: it starts exactly over the button as the bar goes, and grows sideways and settles `Spacing.m` lower while the card rises, the list making `ExpandingSearchBar.room` for it; closing runs that backwards, with the field and the card on the same curve so they land together, and the bar returns just before the field fades into the button. The toolbar's items stay while searching (the bar is just hidden): taken out, they flew off on their own, and the search button gets a new id as search closes, or it comes back still deflating from its press. The field only takes focus once the card is up: loading the keyboard held up the rise. Trailing: filter + `SettingsButton`.
- Every tab has `SettingsButton` (gear → Settings sheet) at the trailing edge. Calendar: Today (`<day>.square`) + settings. Map: Current Location (`location`) + settings. Tabs: Journal, Calendar, Map, Insights.
- Tab bar is icons only (`TabIcon` keeps the name as the accessibility label). The page fades out into the background around and below it (`PageTitle.bottomFade`). It's hidden while an entry is open by the tab's NavigationStack (`.toolbar(path.isEmpty ? .visible : .hidden, for: .tabBar)`), not by the editor: a pushed view's own setting only lifts after its pop animation, so the bar came back late.
- Journal name: `@AppStorage(JournalName.storageKey)`, default "Journal", editable in Settings; shown as the home title and on the lock screen. Use `JournalName.display(_:)` (empty → default).
- New entries: round sand `NewEntryButton` floating bottom-right of the Journal tab (writes for today); the Calendar has none, and neither does its day sheet.
- Appearance is applied by setting `overrideUserInterfaceStyle` on every window (`Appearance.apply`), not `.preferredColorScheme`, so open sheets and the lock window switch together.
- Empty-state and explanatory notes ("Nothing written on this day.", "Deleted entries stay here…", empty-tab descriptions, "No location set") all use `Typography.note` (footnote sans) in secondary text.
- Plain buttons (text, links, rows, cards, icons) use `.buttonStyle(.borderless)`, so they dim while touched like any iOS button. Glass buttons keep `.plain`: their interactive glass already responds.
- Toggles and selection checkmarks are sand (`Theme.accent`). The app-wide tint is primary text, so swipe actions set their own: delete is `Theme.destructive` (red), Restore uses sand.
- Popups listing entries (`DaySheet`, `PlaceEntriesSheet`) share `SheetPage` and `SheetHeader` (`Design/Components.swift`), so their width, spacing, title and label can't drift apart.
- Stone on paper is low contrast: use it only for secondary/meta text, never for body copy.
- Always support Dynamic Type and dark mode.

## Stack
- iOS 18.0+, Swift 6, default actor isolation = MainActor.
- SwiftData for persistence. Keep models CloudKit-compatible (defaults on every property, no `@Attribute(.unique)`, relationships optional) — iCloud sync is planned.
- LocalAuthentication for the optional Face ID lock (`Services/AppLock.swift`). The lock screen lives in its own `UIWindow` above everything (`PrivacyShield`) so sheets and full-screen covers are hidden too; Face ID is only requested once the scene is `.active`.
- WeatherKit (`Services/WeatherFetcher.swift`) — signed with team `L3957C4ZBL`, WeatherKit enabled on the App ID (works in the Simulator too). If requests fail with `WDSJWTAuthenticatorServiceListener.Errors error 2`, the build isn't carrying the team/entitlement. Weather is fetched when a place is set or changed, when the date changes, and when an entry with a place but no weather opens. Entitlement is in `Journal.entitlements`. **Apple's WeatherKit attribution was removed from Settings at the user's request — it must be shown somewhere (e.g. the editor header) before App Store submission.**
- CoreLocation + MapKit for places (`Services/LocationService.swift`).
- No third-party dependencies, no analytics.

## Features
- **Journal tab:** "On this day" section (same `EntryRow`, day + year) above month-grouped entries; rows are plain buttons (no disclosure chevrons). Under each row's text, `EntryMetaLine` shows the time, place and weather ("7:39 AM · Mission District · ☀︎ 14°"; the weather is the entry's stored snapshot, never fetched for a list, and keeps its width while a long place name truncates). Swiping a row left reveals small circle buttons with one short word under each, centered on the entry, the last ending where the separators do and the first `Spacing.m` from the row's highlight (`rowSwipeActions`, `Design/RowSwipe.swift` — the system's swipe actions are a fixed-size capsule; VoiceOver gets `accessibilityTitle` where the word needs more): sand Share (the same plain text as the context menu; it just closes the row) and red Trash, and in Recently Deleted sand Restore and red Delete (permanently). They come out one after the other as the row moves, the rightmost first, and go back in reverse (`SwipeButtons`, Animatable so it's worked out from the row's position every frame; from the final position all of them would animate at once). The swipe is a pan that only starts when mostly sideways, so the list still scrolls, settling like the system's (a critically damped spring measured from it, carrying on at the finger's speed; started on the next turn, as a change made in the gesture's callback isn't animated); rows are softly highlighted while touched and on through a swipe until they close, over the row's full height and the screen's full width (`SwipeRowButtonStyle`, `Radius.row`); only tapping a button acts (no full swipe — dragging further just stretches a little and springs back), and its circle lights up slightly while pressed; a button that takes the row out of the list slides and fades it first (`RowSwipe.act`), then the list closes up (`context.save()` goes inside the `withAnimation`: the save is what updates the `@Query`, so outside it the list collapses unanimated, and the context menu's actions use the same helpers); one row open at a time (`swipedOpen`), closed by scrolling or by a tap, which then doesn't open the entry. A list's context menu covers the whole row wherever `.contextMenu` is applied, so a row swiped open offers none: holding its buttons does nothing. Search ("Search by text, date, or tag") via `Entry.matches`: text/tags/place by substring (image tokens excluded), dates by whole token ("Oct 6" ≠ Oct 16). Filter = All Entries | Recently Deleted (soft delete via `deletedAt`, purged after 30 days on launch), a flat list with the fewest days left first, under one pinned label with the number of entries (the journal's `pinnedSectionLabels` and `entryListRow`, with a single section), and no bulk actions: each entry is restored or deleted from its swipe actions or context menu. Its rows end in a red "N days left" (`EntryRow`'s `warning`, part of the text column so the date centers on all of it). No summary line.
- **Editor:** `Entry.text` is **Markdown**, but the editor shows formatted text with no visible syntax (no `#`, `**`, `>`). `MarkdownDocument` (`Views/Editor/MarkdownStyling.swift`) converts Markdown ⇄ attributed text; semantic attributes (`.inlineStyle`, `.linkURL`, `.blockStyle`) are the source of truth and `MarkdownStyler` derives fonts/colors from them. Lists keep a visible marker (`•`, `☐`, `☑︎`, or the number) that serializes back to `- `, `- [ ] `, `- [x] `. Photos are `![](image:<Attachment.identifier>)` tokens rendered as `ImageAttachment`s; `---` is a `DividerAttachment`. Formatting comes **only** from the toolbar: typed `#`, `**`, `> ` etc. stay literal text — the serializer backslash-escapes any literal character that would otherwise read back as syntax (only where it could: `5 * 3` and `snake_case` stay as typed), and `Markdown.plainText` unescapes. Typing `#tag` makes a tag (styled stone). The header (date/time, place + weather icon and temperature) is a hosted SwiftUI view inside the text view, so the whole page scrolls together; tapping the date opens `DateEditSheet`, sized to fit the calendar and time row. The editor's back button has no long-press menu (`hidesBackButtonMenu()`), so it never shows the journal name. Entries open and close with the plain push slide (no zoom). While editing, a sand checkmark to the right of the ⋯ (top right) ends editing. Its ⋯ menu's Share exports the entry as a PDF (`JournalExport.writePDF(of:)`: `JournalPDF` with no title page, named after the entry's title or date) through `ShareSheet`; the list's context-menu Share still shares plain text. Autocorrect and keyboard suggestions keep the replaced word's formatting (the coordinator carries its inline style and link over to the replacement). A new entry starts in the Title style (the first line is its title). Keyboard bar (`MarkdownToolbar`): a rounded glass capsule floating just above the keyboard (inset from the sides), large icons that scroll sideways when they don't fit: photo, text style popover (Title / Heading / Regular, each set in its editor font with the current one checked — a popover because menu items can't take custom fonts; the aA button itself never highlights), bold, italic, strike, quote, link (asks for a URL), divider — tools get a glass highlight when that style is on at the caret or across the selection (`MarkdownEditorController.activeInline/activeBlock`). Existing `code` spans still display but there's no button. Links have a faint underline; tapping one opens it when not editing (while editing, a tap places the caret). Return continues lists (ends them on an empty item), leaves a title/heading for body text, and leaves a quote on an empty line; tapping ☐ ticks it; tapping a photo opens the viewer. The entry's title (`Entry.title`, shown in list previews) is its first line **only if that line is in the Title style** (`# `); otherwise there's no title and the preview is all excerpt. A photo-only entry shows no stand-in text in lists (just its thumbnail and meta); only a truly blank one says "No content yet.". `#hashtags` = tags. Deleting a photo removes it on exit.
- **Cover image:** `Entry.coverImageID` (falls back to the first embedded photo) → `Entry.coverPhoto`. Set it from the photo viewer's ⋯ menu, which holds both "Set as Cover Image" (same label with a checkmark when it's already the cover) and Delete Photo (asks in an alert with Cancel — a confirmation dialog's glass took on the photo's colors; removes the token from the text via `MarkdownEditorController.removeImage`). The viewer's close and ⋯ buttons are dark glass circles; tapping the black around the photo also closes it, and so does swiping down at normal size (the photo follows the finger and the entry fades in behind — the cover has a clear `presentationBackground`). Photos pinch- and double-tap-zoom (`ZoomableImage`, a `UIScrollView`); its swipe-down pan only begins for a mostly-downward pan when not zoomed, so sideways paging and zoomed panning still work.
- **Calendar tab:** one endless-feeling vertical scroll of months (five centuries either way; only the months within a screen of the view are built). Every month's place is worked out from its week count (`MonthWeeks`, `MonthLayout`) rather than measured, so any month can be placed without building those above it — a `LazyVStack` estimated the heights of unbuilt months and the scroll jumped when the estimates changed, and growing the range as the scroll neared an end moved the month on screen. It opens on this month once the width is known, and keeps the top month in place when the width changes; Today scrolls back to this month. Tapping the Calendar tab while it's open does nothing, even mid-scroll: the top is five centuries back, so `NoScrollToTop` turns off the status bar's scroll to the top (`scrollsToTop`, which the tab's ignores) and refuses that tap in a stand-in for SwiftUI's tab bar delegate (`OpenTabTapIgnorer`, which passes everything else on). Each month's title (a `MetaLabel`, like the editor's date, with the year) (no band behind it) over rounded-square days with semibold sans numbers, the title's row as far from the days on either side as the days are from each other — sand when written, or the cover photo of the day's earliest entry with one (`ThumbnailCache`). The weekday row sits above the scroll at the top of the card (not in its `safeAreaInset`, which made the opening position land this month's title under it). Tapping a day opens `DaySheet` (the day stays dimmed as if pressed while it's open, easing back as the sheet closes — `DayCellStyle`, no outline) (`Views/Calendar/DaySheet.swift`, medium/large detents, swipe down to close): the date ("October 28, 2026" over the weekday) with arrows to the day before/after, then `surfaceCard`s (the look of Insights' tiles): one per entry, unheaded, with no day column (`EntryRow`'s `.none` style, so the time is only on the meta line; tapping one slides the full editor in from the right over the sheet, which stays put: `EntryCover`, a clear `fullScreenCover` shown unanimated with the editor pushed onto an empty stack inside it, so the sheet shows through and swipe-back works; it goes once the slide back ends, in the editor's `onDisappear`, as the path empties when that slide starts), then one for the photos taken that day from the photo library (`Services/PhotoLibrary.swift`: PhotoKit, read access requested from a button the first time, `NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription`). The arrows slide the date and the cards sideways together, right across the sheet (`slidingDay`); the days either side have their photo thumbnails fetched ahead (`PhotoLibrary.preloadThumbnails`, cached), so a card slides in with its photos rather than fading them in on the way (a day's photo fetch is cached too, `PhotoLibrary.photos(on:)`, until the library changes, as the cards are rebuilt on every update).
- **Map tab:** every entry with a place as a numbered sand dot on an `MKMapView` (`Views/Map/EntryMap.swift` — SwiftUI's `Map` can't cluster), opening at once on the current location (its neighbourhood, with the system's dot in Apple Maps' blue, `UITheme.userLocation`; `LocationService.refreshLastKnownLocation` gets it ready on launch, without ever asking, and the first open asks for access) or, without one, fitted to all of them; the bar's arrow goes back to the current location (`EntryMap.recenterCount`), asking for access the first time and saying so in an alert if it's off. Nearby entries group into one dot (`clusteringIdentifier`); every dot is the same size and shows its count, even a lone entry's 1. Tapping a dot opens its entry; tapping a group never zooms, it lists them in `PlaceEntriesSheet` (titled, centered, with their place, or the most common one when a group spans several; rows use `EntryRow`'s `.dayAndMonth` style, highlighted when touched like the journal's, from hairline to hairline across the sheet). An entry tapped there opens inside the sheet (its own `NavigationStack`), which grows to `.large` and can't be swiped away, and going back returns to the list at its size; the list is live, so an entry trashed from there leaves it, and the sheet closes when none are left. Detent changes go on the turn after the selection change: in the same update the sheet stays put. `presentationDetents` and `sheetGrabber()` sit outside the stack, as a pushed page's `SheetBackdrop` would fade the backdrop out. Dots are tapped through `Coordinator.tap(_:)`, not MapKit's selection, which holds a tap ~0.5 s to rule out a double tap: selection is off on every dot and the location dot (`isEnabled = false`), since a tap would otherwise fall through to whatever's below and open the location dot's avatar callout. Long-pressing the map starts an entry there, named by reverse geocoding ("Dropped Pin" if that fails); the editor gets `placeIsProvisional`, so like an auto-added place it's discarded if nothing else is added (and the "Add current location" setting doesn't look up a place for an entry that already has one).
- **Insights tab:** a words tile spanning the top, then streak / longest streak / entries / days written in a 2×2 grid (no heading), then "Writing activity": an 18-week activity grid (written / not written — `Theme.activityFilled`/`activityEmpty`, no legend or summary line; tapping a day shows its date and word count). No tags section (removed deliberately).
- **Settings:** every row has an icon but the journal name. Journal name, appearance (a Theme row showing the choice, opening a list of system/light/dark with a sand checkmark), daily reminder (it turns on even with notifications off in iOS, with a note linking to iOS's Settings, and `ReminderScheduler.refresh` schedules it once the app comes back with them on), "Add current location" (`LocationService.autoTagKey`; turning it on asks for location access and stays off if refused — the editor never prompts: a fresh entry dated today gets the current place and its weather on open, and if it's left with no text or photos the auto-added place is cleared so the entry is still discarded), Face ID lock, Export (button → Markdown or PDF dialog → share sheet). `JournalExport.write` builds the file; `JournalPDF` typesets a book-style PDF (title page, entries via TextKit with formatted Markdown and photos, page numbers, entries starting near a page foot move to the next page).
- **Location sheet** ("Set Location"): custom search field pre-filled with the current place, a non-interactive map as wide as the cards below it (camera set in `init` — the Map ignores changes made in `onAppear`) marking the place with a sand dot on a soft sand halo, then Current Location / Remove / place suggestions; sections are `Spacing.m` apart, like the search field and map.
- No favorites, no writing prompts, no mood (all removed deliberately).
- **App Shortcut:** "New entry in Journal" (`App/NewEntryIntent.swift`) via `AppRouter.shared`.

## Project layout
- `Journal.xcodeproj` uses a file-system-synchronized group: any file added under `Journal/` is in the target automatically — no need to edit `project.pbxproj`.
- `App` entry point, tabs, lock handling, router, intents · `Models` SwiftData models (`Entry`, `Attachment`) · `Design` theme tokens + shared components · `Views/<Feature>` screens · `Services` non-UI logic (Markdown helpers, stats, export, reminders, location, weather, image processing).
- Queries for live entries must filter `deletedAt == nil`. Use `entry.moveToTrash()` rather than `context.delete` for user-facing deletes.
- Blank entries are created eagerly on "New entry"; the editor deletes them on exit, and `Entry.deleteBlankEntries` sweeps leftovers on launch and when a navigation stack returns to root (skipping `AppRouter.openEntries`).
- Save explicitly (`try? context.save()`) after leaving the editor, destructive actions, and when the scene leaves `.active` — don't rely on autosave timing for a journal.
- Editor clipboard: copy/cut put **Markdown** (with photo tokens) on the pasteboard; paste parses Markdown (so formatting carries over) and rebuilds photos inline (`resolvePastedImage` copies photos from other entries, and duplicates get their own `Attachment`), images from other apps are imported, and photos can't be dragged. Pasted photos always get their own line.
- Live styling is incremental (`MarkdownStyler.style(_:in:)` restyles only edited paragraphs, tracked via `NSTextStorageDelegate`) — keep every styling rule line-local, and keep `MarkdownDocument` round-tripping (Markdown → editor → Markdown must reproduce stored entries exactly; formatting changes only touch attributes). Format toggles register their own undo (`editAttributes`); edits that insert attachments clear the undo stack. Rendered photos are cached by id (`ImageAttachment.cached`).
- `SystemPrompts.shared.during { … }` wraps any permission prompt (Face ID, location, notifications) so the privacy shield doesn't flash up while the scene is briefly inactive.
- Journal name is capped at `JournalName.maxLength` (40).
- The editor takes the keyboard only once its push transition has finished (`MarkdownTextView.focusWhenReady` waits on the navigation controller's `transitionCoordinator`), so the keyboard rises from the bottom instead of sliding in with the page.
- Don't push onto a NavigationStack while `.searchable` is dismissing (the push gets dropped); `EntriesView.newEntry` closes search first.

## Debugging
- Launch with `-seedSampleData` (DEBUG only) to fill an empty store with ~4 months of entries:
  `xcrun simctl launch booted com.maxsun.journal -seedSampleData`
- Simulator Face ID: enroll with `xcrun simctl spawn booted notifyutil -s com.apple.BiometricKit.enrollmentChanged 1 && xcrun simctl spawn booted notifyutil -p com.apple.BiometricKit.enrollmentChanged`; pass a prompt with `xcrun simctl spawn booted notifyutil -p com.apple.BiometricKit_Sim.pearl.match` (`.nomatch` to fail).
- Simulated taps on iOS 26 switches need a ~0.2s press to register. Injected taps right after a launch, or very short ones, are often dropped by the simulator — use ~0.15s taps and don't read a missed first tap as an app bug.
- Launch with `-lockEnabled NO` to skip the Face ID lock for one run without changing the stored setting.
- Read the store directly: `sqlite3 "$(xcrun simctl get_app_container booted com.maxsun.journal data)/Library/Application Support/default.store" "select ZTEXT from ZENTRY"`.
